## Problem

Fresco is a 7-inch networked touchscreen control station for commercial and institutional lighting environments. It unifies disparate control protocols: traditional line-voltage dimming, 0-10V, DALI, nLight devices (up to 128 units), and DMX512-A/RDM for dynamic RGB and tunable white LEDs. This creates a single, cohesive user experience.

The lighting industry had competing touchscreen controllers with fragmented UX and inflexible interfaces. Acuity needed a platform that could bridge proprietary lighting networks while remaining intuitive for electricians, facility managers, and lighting designers installing and configuring systems across commercial buildings, hospitality venues, retail spaces, and institutions.

## Role

Owned the complete product flow end to end:

- **Industrial design**: Physical enclosure, bezel form, mounting considerations, cable management.
- **Interface design**: Navigation architecture, channel control, color selection, scene management, settings hierarchy.
- **Packaging design**: Shipping box, unboxing experience, first-impression branding.
- **Installation and marketing materials**: Quick-start guides, field documentation, trade show demo case, technical collateral.

I designed features within an existing product concept; I did not originate the business requirements or platform vision.

## The decision

Three design decisions across disciplines shaped the product:

**1. Interface simplicity through constraint.** The core screen prioritizes six-channel room-level control with percentage readouts and independent sliders. Advanced features like color selection, scene editing, and scheduling are accessible but secondary. Operators at all skill levels can understand the lighting state at a glance.

**2. Hardware-informed industrial design.** The physical enclosure integrated cable routing, thermal management, and mounting flexibility into a compact footprint. The bezel and screen size were validated against typical viewing distances and touch accuracy requirements. The form was engineered for both wall and desktop mounting without accessory kits.

**3. Scaffolded onboarding through materials.** Installation guides, packaging graphics, and demo case were designed as a continuous narrative. From unboxing through network configuration to field deployment. The demo case itself was 3D-modeled to showcase product capability and serve as a portable sales and testing tool.

## Reasoning

A lighting control station is a bridge between hardware and human behavior. Acuity's opportunity was not to invent novel UI patterns. It was to bring clarity to a fragmented market by treating the product as a complete system. Physical, digital, and experiential together.

The six-channel interface reduces cognitive load for 95% of common use cases (room-level dimming and color). Advanced users can drill into granular controls for complex setups. This stratification is visible in the information architecture and in the physical product itself. The screen is prominent. The enclosure is understated.

Packaging and installation materials often get designed last. Here they were co-designed alongside the interface and hardware. The result is a product that arrives and installs with less friction. An operational detail that matters at deployment scale.

## Outcome

Fresco launched at Lightfair International in Las Vegas in 2014. The system was positioned as the first lighting control platform to unlock the full capability of dynamic and efficient sources across hospitality, museums, retail, conference spaces, and institutional buildings.

The product earned the LFI Innovation Award in the Lighting Controls category at Lightfair International 2015. Recognition came from an independent panel of lighting professionals.

The ecosystem expanded with Fresco Mobile (iOS app for wireless control), Fresco Studio (configuration software with BACnet/IP building management integration), and continuous feature releases. By 2018, the platform supported up to 288 controlled zones with occupancy sensing and daylight harvesting. Development remained active through at least December 2018, with version 200.50 addressing nLight library updates and DMX fixture compatibility.

The product's success came from treating disparate disciplines as one problem. The interface, the enclosure, the packaging, and the field experience all reinforced the same design principles. Fresco remains in Acuity Brands' active product portfolio as an architectural control platform.
